QLD Licence Renewal Guide · 2026-27

Renewing your QLD trade licence

Queensland has two regulators for trades, revenue-based fees for contractors, and an annual financial reporting duty. Here's what each trade pays and what to check first.

Fees checked against WorkSafe Queensland and QBCC on 13 November 2026.

Who you renew with

Electricians deal with Queensland's Electrical Safety Office (fees are listed on WorkSafe.qld.gov.au). It issues both the electrical work licence, which lets you do the work, and the electrical contractor licence, which lets you run a business doing it.

Builders, trade contractors and plumbers renew with the Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC).

Queensland fees rose on 1 July 2026 by a Government Indexation Rate of 3.4%.

A sole-trader electrician with both licences pays $561.45 to renew them. Most of the contractor fee is an administration component ($368.45), which is refundable in limited cases. The renewal fee and the reinstatement fee are the same, but working while a licence has lapsed is still unlicensed work, so don't treat that as a reason to wait.

Plumbers and drainers

QBCC licenses plumbing and drainage workers through occupational licences. You can renew for 1 to 5 years. The fees below include QBCC's admin fee of $80.90, charged once per financial year per licensee.

Worked example: five 1-year renewals cost $847.20. One 5-year renewal costs $523.73, which saves $323.47. Almost half of the 1-year price is the $80.90 admin fee, so longer terms work out much cheaper in Queensland. 5 years comes to $104.75 a year against $169.44.

If you also run a plumbing business, you'll usually need a QBCC contractor licence as well. Contractor fees follow the revenue tiers below.

Builders and trade contractors: maximum revenue tiers

QBCC contractor fees depend on your maximum revenue (MR): the most your business may turn over in a year under that licence. Pick the tier that fits your real turnover. If you outgrow your tier, you're working outside your licence.

QBCC's contractor fee page is still dated 1 July 2025. QBCC raises fees every 1 July, and other Queensland fees rose 3.4% this year, so your 2026-27 notice may be a little higher. Companies pay more, for example $836.55 for 1 year at SC2. A licence restoration costs $142.70 on top of the renewal fee.

The 3-year term is the better deal on every tier. At SC2 it's $356.33 a year against $419.21, which saves $188.64 over three years.

Minimum Financial Requirements (MFR)

Many QBCC contractor licences carry Minimum Financial Requirements: you report on your business finances each year, separately from paying the renewal fee. What you report depends on your licence category and revenue tier. Check your obligation with QBCC well before renewal time, because outstanding MFR reporting can hold up your licence even after the fee is paid.

How to renew, step by step

  1. List every licence you hold. Electricians often have two with the Electrical Safety Office. Contractors may also hold an occupational or supervisor licence with QBCC.
  2. Check your revenue tier against last year's actual turnover before renewing a contractor licence.
  3. Bring your MFR reporting up to date if your licence class needs it.
  4. Choose your term where there's a choice. Longer terms are cheaper per year in QLD.
  5. Renew before the expiry date and keep the receipt. A restored licence costs an extra $142.70 with QBCC.
