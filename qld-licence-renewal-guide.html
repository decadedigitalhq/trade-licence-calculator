QLD Licence Renewal Guide

Renewing your QLD trade licence, step by step

Queensland licensing has its own quirks — from Minimum Financial Requirements reporting to Maximum Revenue tiers for builders. Here's what actually happens at renewal time.

Who regulates what in Queensland

The Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC) handles licensing for builders and for plumber & drainer contractor licences. Electricians sit across two bodies: Electrical Safety Queensland (part of the Office of Industrial Relations) issues the electrical worker licence you need to do the physical work, while QBCC issues the separate contractor licence you need to run an electrical contracting business. Most licensed electricians running their own business end up dealing with both at different points.

Plumber & drainer renewals are published flat rates for 1–5 year terms. Builder fees scale with your Maximum Revenue tier rather than a single flat number — see below.

Maximum Revenue tiers for builders

QBCC builder licence fees are set according to a "Maximum Revenue" tier — effectively a declared ceiling on the annual revenue your licence permits you to earn. Lower tiers cost less to renew but also cap how much work you can take on; if your business has grown since your last renewal, staying in a tier that no longer matches your actual turnover can put you outside your licence conditions, not just cost you more at renewal.

Minimum Financial Requirements (MFR) reporting

Many QBCC licence categories require annual Minimum Financial Requirements reporting alongside your renewal — a separate financial reporting obligation, not just the renewal fee itself. Exactly what's required (a declaration, or more detailed financial statements) depends on your licence category and Maximum Revenue tier, so it's worth checking your specific reporting obligation on the QBCC website well before your renewal is due, rather than discovering it at the deadline.

If your licence lapses

Working on an expired QBCC or electrical licence in Queensland is illegal and can void your insurance. QBCC charges a late renewal fee on top of the standard amount, and if your MFR reporting isn't current, that alone can hold up a renewal even if the fee itself has been paid.

Common mistakes worth avoiding

The two QLD-specific issues we see most: treating MFR reporting as optional or assuming it's bundled into the renewal fee (it isn't, and missing it can delay your renewal), and not reviewing your Maximum Revenue tier as your business grows, which can leave you technically outside your licence conditions even with a fully paid-up renewal.
