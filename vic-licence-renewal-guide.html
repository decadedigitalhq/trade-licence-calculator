VIC Licence Renewal Guide · 2026-27

Renewing your VIC trade licence

Victoria has two regulators, different terms for every licence, and one late-fee rule that catches builders out. Here's what each trade pays for 2026-27.

Fees checked against Energy Safe Victoria and the Building and Plumbing Commission on 13 November 2026.

Who you renew with

Electricians renew with Energy Safe Victoria (ESV). Plumbers and builders renew with the Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC), the trading name of what was the Victorian Building Authority. If you hold both an electrical licence and a building registration, you deal with both bodies, on different dates.

Victorian fees are set from a "fee unit" the Treasurer fixes each year: $17.27 for 2026-27, up from $16.81. You don't need to do that sum yourself. Both regulators now publish the dollar amount for each year.

The Electrician's licence (A) lets you do the work. The REC is what you need to contract for electrical work in your own name. A sole trader usually holds both: $588.40 every 5 years, or about $118 a year. That's one of the cheapest trade licences in the country.

Don't let it lapse: renewing costs $235.70, but a fresh application for a licence (A) costs $471.50 and an REC costs $734.30. ESV says the renew option appears in your online account three months before expiry. If yours has already expired, contact ESV licensing straight away.

Victorian plumbers can hold a registration, a licence, or both. They're on different cycles: registration renews every 3 years and the licence every 12 months. A plumber holding both pays $416.30 every third year plus $369.00 every year. Over 3 years that's $1,523.30, or about $508 a year.

The late renewal fee for plumbers is small ($91.30), but it's added each time you miss a date. With two cycles to track, it's easy to pay it more than once.

Builders (Building and Plumbing Commission)

Victorian builders pay two different things: an annual fee every year, and a renewal fee when the registration comes up for renewal (every 5 years). The BPC sends an email reminder six weeks before your annual fee is due.

Companies pay more. For example, a domestic builder (unlimited) company pays a $727.30 annual fee and a $1,600.20 renewal fee. The BPC also lists discounted fees for some categories. Your notice shows which ones apply to you.

The late-fee trap: the BPC's $342.80 late fee (individuals, 2026-27) applies to renewals lodged less than 3 months before your registration expiry date. You can be charged a late fee while your registration is still current. Lodge the renewal more than 3 months out. For a company, the late fee is $480.00.

Paying the annual fee isn't the only condition. Most building practitioners must give the BPC updated proof of the required insurance every year. If either the fee or the insurance is overdue, the BPC must suspend your registration. Domestic builder insurance rules changed from 1 July 2026, so check what applies to you.

Struggling to pay?

The BPC has financial hardship relief for building practitioners. Grounds include natural disaster, loss of income, business financial difficulty, family violence and carer responsibilities. Depending on the fee, relief can mean an exempt annual fee, an exempt late fee, or half off a registration application. You'll need supporting evidence, such as a letter from your accountant.

How to renew, step by step

  1. Work out which body and which item is due. ESV for electrical, BPC for plumbing and building. Plumbers and builders may have both an annual item and a longer renewal.
  2. Check your insurance is current before you start. Builders must upload proof to the BPC every year.
  3. Log in and pay. For BPC, pay on the Registration and Licensing page of your BPC account. For ESV, use your online account when the renew option appears.
  4. Lodge early. For builders, more than 3 months before expiry avoids the late fee.
  5. Keep the receipt and your updated licence or registration details. Head contractors often ask for them.
