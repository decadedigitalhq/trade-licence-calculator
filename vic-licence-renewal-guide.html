VIC Licence Renewal Guide

Renewing your VIC trade licence, step by step

Victoria doesn't publish flat renewal fees the way NSW does — here's what the "fee unit" system actually means, who regulates your trade, and how to avoid the most common renewal mix-ups.

Who regulates trade licensing in Victoria

Electricians renew their licence through Energy Safe Victoria, the state's electrical safety regulator. Plumbers and builders renew through the Building and Plumbing Commission (VIC), which oversees registration and licensing for building-related trades. If you hold more than one licence type — for example, an electrical contractor licence and a builder registration — you may be dealing with both bodies on different renewal cycles.

How the fee unit system works

Rather than publishing a flat dollar figure per licence class, Victoria calculates most regulatory fees — including trade licence renewals — using a "fee unit" value that's set by the Victorian Government and indexed annually, multiplied by a rate specific to your licence or registration category. That's why you won't find a simple published number for VIC renewals the way you can for NSW: the actual dollar cost depends on both the current fee unit value for the financial year and which category you sit in. This also means your renewal cost can shift each year even if your licence category hasn't changed, simply because the fee unit itself has been re-indexed.

When and how to renew

Renewal notices are generally issued electronically or by post ahead of your registration or licence expiry, and most renewals are completed through the relevant regulator's online portal rather than by phone or in person. It's worth renewing as soon as your notice arrives rather than waiting until close to the deadline, since portal processing and payment confirmation can take a few business days.

What you'll need

You'll typically need a current Certificate of Currency for your insurance, confirmation of the current financial year's fee unit rate (your regulator's renewal notice will usually show this calculated for you), and, for registered builders, evidence that your registration category and any required insurance are still current and correctly matched to the work you're doing.

If your licence or registration lapses

As with every state, working on an expired electrical licence or building registration in Victoria is not permitted and can put your insurance cover at risk. Reinstating a lapsed registration typically costs more than a standard on-time renewal, and a longer lapse can mean the regulator treats it as a fresh application.

Common mistakes worth avoiding

The most common VIC-specific mix-up is assuming last year's dollar amount still applies — because the underlying fee unit is re-indexed annually, the actual cost changes even when nothing about your licence has. The second is renewing with the wrong regulator: since electricians go through Energy Safe Victoria while plumbers and builders go through the Building and Plumbing Commission, it's worth double-checking which body actually issued your current licence before you start the renewal.
