Frequently asked questions

Do I need insurance to hold a trade licence?

In most states, yes — public liability insurance is either a legal condition of your licence or a practical requirement, since most building sites, principal contractors, and councils won't let you start work without a valid Certificate of Currency. Some licence categories (like registered building practitioners in several states) also require Professional Indemnity insurance. Comparison platforms like BizCover let you quote across multiple insurers in one go rather than calling around individually.

Why don't you have an exact fee for every state and trade?

We only publish figures we can verify directly from official regulator fee schedules. Some states (like Victoria) calculate fees using formulas rather than flat published rates, and some trades (like QLD builders) scale by business turnover. Where we can't verify an exact number, we link you straight to the official source instead of guessing.

Do these fees include GST?

Most Australian government licensing fees are GST-exempt, including the ones listed here. Always confirm on your renewal notice, as this can vary by fee component.

What happens if I miss my renewal deadline?

In every state, working on an expired licence is illegal and can invalidate your insurance. Most regulators charge a restoration fee on top of the normal renewal fee if you're late, and if it's been expired too long (often 3+ months) you may need to reapply from scratch rather than simply restore it.

Can I renew for a shorter or longer period to save money?

Where multi-year options exist (like NSW's 1/3/5-year choices), longer renewal periods work out cheaper per year, but require more money upfront. Weigh that against how settled you are in your current business structure.

Do fees change every year?

Yes. Most regulators (NSW, QLD) increase fees annually on 1 July in line with CPI or legislated schedules. We update this page when new fee schedules are published, but always cross-check your exact renewal notice for the final figure.
