NSW Licence Renewal Guide

Renewing your NSW trade licence, step by step

A step-by-step look at what happens when you renew an electrician, plumber or builder licence in New South Wales — not just what it costs, but who you're dealing with and what to have ready.

Who regulates what in NSW

NSW trade licensing sits across two connected bodies. NSW Fair Trading administers the licensing system directly — issuing licences, processing renewals, and running the public licence check register. Building Commission NSW oversees compliance for higher-risk residential building work, including a growing share of builder and specialist licence categories following the state's building reforms. Depending on your trade and licence class, your renewal notice may come from either body — check the letterhead if you're not sure who to contact about a specific renewal.

When to renew

NSW licences are typically issued for 1, 3, or 5-year terms, and renewal notices are usually sent several weeks before your expiry date. It's worth renewing before the expiry date rather than after — working on an expired licence isn't just a compliance risk, it can also affect whether your insurer will cover a claim.

What you'll typically need

Most renewals require a current Certificate of Currency for your public liability insurance, up-to-date business and contact details, and payment of the renewal fee for your chosen term. If you're a licensed builder doing residential building work above the mandatory threshold, you'll also need current Home Building Compensation cover in place before the renewal can be finalised — this is separate from your public liability policy.

If you renew late or let it lapse

Working on an expired licence is illegal in NSW and can invalidate your insurance cover. If you renew late, expect a restoration fee on top of the standard renewal amount. If a licence has been expired for an extended period — often several months — NSW Fair Trading may require a fresh application rather than a simple restoration, which takes longer and can mean re-demonstrating eligibility requirements you'd already met the first time around.

What it actually costs

Electrician, 1 year$403.00
Plumber, 1 year$388.00
Builder, 1 year$849.00

Multi-year terms are available for all three and work out cheaper per year. Use the fee calculator for the full breakdown by term length.

Common mistakes worth avoiding

The most frequent renewal hiccups we hear about aren't about the money — they're process issues: letting the insurance Certificate of Currency lapse before the licence renewal is processed, renewing under the wrong licence subcategory (NSW has several tiers within each trade), and assuming last year's fee still applies. NSW fee schedules are typically updated on 1 July, so a figure from your previous renewal notice may already be out of date.

Disclaimer: this guide explains the general renewal process and is not legal or financial advice. Requirements vary by exact licence class and can change — always confirm your specific requirements with NSW Fair Trading or Building Commission NSW before renewing.
