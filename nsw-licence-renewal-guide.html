NSW Licence Renewal Guide · 2026-27

Renewing your NSW trade licence

What an electrician, plumber or builder pays to renew in NSW this financial year, which term gives you the best value, and what happens if you miss the date.

Fees checked against Building Commission NSW on 13 November 2026 (their page was updated 1 September 2026).

The short answer

An individual electrician or plumber pays $299 to renew for 1 year, $715 for 3 years or $1,231 for 5 years. A builder pays $592, $1,422 or $2,475. If you only hold a qualified supervisor or tradesperson certificate (you work for someone else and don't contract in your own name), it's just $33, $66 or $113.

Who you renew with

Building and trade licences in NSW are issued by Building Commission NSW. You may still see NSW Fair Trading on older paperwork and on the public licence check, because Fair Trading ran the system before the Building Commission took it over.

NSW sorts licences into "building work" and "trade or specialist work". Electrical wiring, plumbing, draining and gasfitting, and air conditioning and refrigeration are all specialist work. That matters because the two groups have different fees. If you're an electrician or plumber, you're paying the trade or specialist rate.

Which term is the best value?

Most people assume the 5-year option is the cheapest per year. In NSW it isn't. Here's the cost per year for an individual licence:

Cost per year by term (individual, 2026-27)
Licence1 year3 years5 years
Trade or specialist$299$238$246
Building contractor$592$474$495

Worked example: an electrician renewing three times on a 1-year licence pays $897. A single 3-year renewal costs $715, which saves $182. Picking 5 years instead of 3 costs about $8 a year more, but you only deal with the renewal once in five years. For some people that's worth $40.

Fees rise with CPI at the start of each financial year, so a longer term also locks in today's price for longer. If you're winding down, changing structure (sole trader to company), or think you may leave NSW, the shorter term makes more sense.

If you renew late

NSW doesn't add a separate late fee. Instead, if your licence has expired and you lodge within 3 months of the expiry date, you pay a restoration fee in place of the renewal fee:

Individual restoration fees, 2026-27
Licence1 year3 years5 years
Trade or specialist contractor$367$785$1,388
Building contractor$661$1,492$2,658

So being late on a 5-year trade licence costs you $157 extra. Wait longer than 3 months and you may need to apply as a new applicant. A new 1-year trade licence application is $403, and you'd be proving your qualifications again.

The bigger cost is usually not the fee. Contracting on an expired licence is unlicensed work, and that can affect your insurance and your right to be paid.

How to renew, step by step

  1. Check your expiry date and licence classes on your licence card or with the NSW licence check. Make sure every class you actually work in is on it.
  2. Choose your term (1, 3 or 5 years). See the cost-per-year table above.
  3. Get your paperwork ready. If you do residential building work above the threshold, have your Home Building Compensation cover current. This is separate from public liability insurance.
  4. Lodge the renewal before the expiry date using the link on your renewal notice, and pay the fee.
  5. Keep the confirmation. Builders and head contractors often ask for proof that your licence is current before you start on site.

Adding a class while you renew

Picked up a new skill? Adding a category to an individual licence is a separate fee: $195 to add a trade or specialist category, or $382 to add a building category. Doing it at renewal time means your licence card is up to date in one go.

Common mistakes

Paying last year's amount. NSW fees rise each July, so an old notice is out of date. Renewing the wrong licence type: if you contract in your own name you need the contractor licence, not just the supervisor certificate. And letting it run past 3 months, which turns a $68 restoration premium into a full new application.
